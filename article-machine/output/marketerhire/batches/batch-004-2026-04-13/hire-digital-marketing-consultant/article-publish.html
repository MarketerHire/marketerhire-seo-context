What Does a Digital Marketing Consultant Do?

A digital marketing consultant owns the strategy and execution of your online marketing channels — paid search, paid social, SEO, email, content, and analytics — either hands-on or by managing specialists who do the channel work. They differ from an in-house marketing manager in one critical way: they bring cross-company pattern recognition from working with dozens of businesses, not just yours.

Core responsibilities typically include:

  • Growth strategy development — auditing your current channels, identifying the highest-ROI opportunities, and building a 90-day plan
  • Channel management — running or overseeing paid media, SEO, email marketing, and organic social
  • Analytics and reporting — setting up attribution, building dashboards, and translating data into decisions
  • Campaign execution — launching and optimizing campaigns across platforms (Google Ads, Meta, LinkedIn, etc.)
  • Vendor oversight — managing agencies, freelancers, or tools on your behalf and holding them accountable to KPIs

The key difference from an agency: a consultant is dedicated to your business. You are not one of 15 accounts managed by a rotating cast of junior staff. As one MarketerHire customer put it: "One thing I've found in the marketing stuff is it seems everybody says they can do everything." A good consultant does not claim to do everything. They tell you which channels matter for your business and execute on those.

Signs You Need to Hire a Digital Marketing Consultant

You should hire a digital marketing consultant when your business has growth targets that your current team or setup cannot meet — and hiring a full-time specialist is either too slow, too expensive, or too risky for what you need right now.

Specific signals that it is time:

  • Your growth has stalled and you do not know why. Revenue flatlined, pipeline dried up, and nobody on the team can diagnose the problem. "What we're doing isn't working. I need someone who can come and say, here's what I think you actually need to be focusing on." — MHM LIVING discovery call.
  • You have no in-house marketing expertise. Common in post-acquisition companies and seed-stage startups. "In this business, no one in this company has considered a paid advertising strategy, let alone bought an ad or pulled together a search term strategy." — Centre Partners discovery call.
  • Agency burnout. You have spent six figures on agencies and the results are thin. "I've been through multiple different marketing agencies." — 409 Group. A freelance digital marketing consultant gives you senior talent working directly on your account, not a junior coordinator behind an account manager.
  • Headcount freeze but growing targets. The board will not approve a $150K full-time hire, but pipeline targets just increased. A consultant on a monthly retainer fills the gap at 30-50% of the cost.
  • New market or channel entry. You are launching paid search for the first time, expanding to a new geography, or testing TikTok — and nobody on the team has done it before.

If two or more of these apply, you are past the point where a consultant is optional.

How to Hire a Digital Marketing Consultant (Step by Step)

Hiring a digital marketing consultant the right way takes five steps: define scope, choose your sourcing model, evaluate on results, run a trial, and set measurement. Most companies skip steps 1 and 4, which is why 46% of MarketerHire customers have already been burned by a previous marketing hire or agency.

Step 1: Define Your Scope and Goals

Write down what you need before you start looking. Be specific. "We need help with marketing" is not a scope. "We need someone to manage $15K/month in Google Ads spend and reduce CPA from $180 to $120 within 90 days" is a scope.

Questions to answer:

  • Which channels or functions need coverage?
  • What does success look like at 30, 60, and 90 days?
  • How many hours per week do you need?
  • What is your monthly budget for the consultant (separate from ad spend)?

For small businesses hiring a digital marketing consultant for the first time, start narrow. One channel. One goal. Expand after proving results.

Step 2: Choose Your Sourcing Model

Four options, ranked by reliability:

  1. Vetted talent marketplaces (MarketerHire, Toptal) — pre-screened specialists, matched to your needs, fastest time to start
  2. Professional referrals — high trust, but limited pool and slow
  3. LinkedIn search — large pool, but you are doing all the vetting yourself
  4. General freelance platforms (Upwork, Fiverr) — lowest cost, lowest quality floor, highest management overhead

The sourcing model you choose determines how much vetting you have to do yourself. A marketplace with a less-than-5% acceptance rate has already filtered out 95% of candidates. A raw LinkedIn search has not.

Step 3: Evaluate Candidates on Results, Not Resumes

Certifications and job titles tell you almost nothing. Past performance in a similar context tells you almost everything.

What to ask:

  • "Show me a campaign you ran for a company at our stage and budget. What were the results?"
  • "What would your first 30 days look like?"
  • "What channels would you deprioritize for us, and why?"
  • "Walk me through a campaign that failed. What happened and what did you change?"

Red flags: vague answers, no specific metrics, claims of expertise across every channel, unwillingness to share past work. A strong candidate will name specific platforms, budgets, and outcomes without being prompted.

Step 4: Run a Paid Trial Before Committing

This step eliminates 90% of bad hires. A 2-week paid trial lets you see the consultant in action — how they communicate, how fast they move, whether their work quality matches their pitch.

MarketerHire builds this into every engagement: a 2-week trial period, after which 95% of companies choose to continue. If it does not work, you part ways with zero long-term obligation.

Do not skip this. A $2,000 trial is the cheapest insurance against a $50,000 mistake.

Step 5: Set KPIs and a Review Cadence

Before the engagement starts, agree on:

  • 3-5 KPIs tied to business outcomes (not vanity metrics)
  • Weekly async updates and a biweekly review call
  • A 90-day evaluation point with clear success criteria

Good KPIs for a digital marketing consultant depend on the channel. For paid media: cost per acquisition, ROAS, and qualified lead volume. For SEO: organic traffic growth, keyword rankings, and conversion rate. For email: revenue per send, list growth rate, and engagement rates. Avoid vanity metrics like impressions or follower counts unless they are directly tied to a revenue model.

"Success would look like when we go on our scorecard metrics, that we're hitting all the numbers." — Rhino Roofs discovery call. That clarity up front prevents the "this isn't working" conversation at month three.

How Much Does a Digital Marketing Consultant Cost?

Most digital marketing consultants charge between $100 and $300 per hour, or $3,000 to $10,000 per month on a retainer basis. Project-based engagements (audits, launches, strategy builds) typically run $5,000 to $25,000 depending on scope. Your actual cost depends on the consultant's seniority, channel specialization, and the hours you need.

Pricing by engagement type:

  • Hourly: $100-$300/hr. Best for advisory work or short-term projects. A senior consultant at $200/hr working 10 hours per week costs $8,000/month.
  • Monthly retainer: $3,000-$10,000/mo. Most common model for ongoing execution. Includes a set number of hours and defined deliverables.
  • Project-based: $5,000-$25,000 per project. Best for one-time needs — channel audits, go-to-market strategy, marketing tech stack setup.

FAQ — Hiring a Digital Marketing Consultant

What qualifications should a digital marketing consultant have?

Look for 5+ years of hands-on channel experience, a portfolio of measurable results from companies at your stage and budget, and specific platform certifications (Google Ads, Meta Blueprint, HubSpot) where relevant. Generalist credentials matter less than a track record of driving revenue in your channel.

How long does it take to see results from a digital marketing consultant?

Expect initial diagnostics and quick wins within the first 2-4 weeks. Meaningful performance improvements — lower CPA, higher conversion rates, increased pipeline — typically emerge within 60-90 days. Paid channels show results faster than organic channels like SEO or content marketing.

Can a digital marketing consultant work with my existing team?

Yes. Most consultants work alongside your in-house team, filling expertise gaps rather than replacing headcount. A common model: the consultant owns strategy and specialist execution for one or two channels while your team handles day-to-day content, social, and coordination. What is the difference between a digital marketing consultant and a marketing strategist?

A marketing strategist focuses on planning — market positioning, channel selection, messaging frameworks. A digital marketing consultant both plans and executes. They build the strategy and run the campaigns. If you need someone who will actually log into your ad accounts and optimize spend, you want a consultant, not a strategist.

Is hiring a freelance digital marketing consultant worth it for small businesses?

For most small businesses spending $2,000-$10,000 per month on marketing, a freelance digital marketing consultant is the highest-ROI hiring model. You get senior expertise at a fraction of full-time cost, with no long-term commitment. A consultant at $5,000/month who improves your ROAS by 30% on a $10,000 ad budget generates $3,000/month in incremental return — paying for most of their fee.
